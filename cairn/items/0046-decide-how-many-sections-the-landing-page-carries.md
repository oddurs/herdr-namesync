Three new sections are proposed -- one title becoming two names, what the
sidebar actually gets, and what namesync will not do. Adding all three takes
the page from five sections to eight, and a landing page that argues for eight
screens stops being a landing page.

Two of the three can fold instead of standing alone:

- **"Two surfaces, two shapes"** can become a seventh row in the `rules` list
  rather than its own section, at the cost of the two-column visual.
- **"What the sidebar gets"** can become an annotation on the hero mock, which
  is arguably where it belongs anyway -- the mock is already showing `$project`,
  `worktree`, `held` and a duration, and the explanation is currently nowhere.

"What it will not do" does not fold. It is the trust section and it needs its
own quiet.

So the real choice is eight sections with a clean rhythm, or six with two ideas
carried by existing furniture. This blocks 0047 and 0048, which is why it is
its own item rather than a paragraph in one of them.

Worth noting the rhythm argument on the other side: the page today is one
visual and four screens of prose, and each new section is proposed with a
distinct visual form -- table, definition list, two-column diagram, quiet
prose, code. Length is not the only variable.

Done when: the arc is decided and 0047 and 0048 say which form they are taking.

## Decided: seven sections

Neither of the two options as framed. The split is not "eight or six" but which
of the two foldable ideas is actually carried by the furniture it would fold
into.

**"What the sidebar gets" folds into the hero.** It becomes an annotation on
the mock. This is not a compromise -- it is where it belonged from the start.
The mock already renders `$n`, `$project`, `worktree`, `held` and a duration,
and a callout pointing at those beats a paragraph two screens away describing
them from memory. Its own section would have meant drawing the row twice.

**"Two surfaces, two shapes" does not fold.** The proposal was to make it a
seventh row in the `rules` list, and that list answers one question -- *when*
should a name change. The two-surfaces idea answers a different one: what
*shape* does the name take when it does. Filing it as a rule would put an
answer under the wrong question, and it would lose the two-column visual, which
is the most demonstrable thing on the page: one title, two names, no prose
needed.

So: hero (annotated), why another renamer, nothing generates the name, when it
renames, two surfaces, what it will not do, install. Seven.

The rhythm this produces is the argument for it -- mock, table, terminal,
definition list, two-column diagram, quiet prose, code. No two adjacent
sections share a form, which is the thing the page did not have when this
milestone opened.

0047 takes its own section. 0048 becomes a hero annotation.
